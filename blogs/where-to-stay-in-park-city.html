Where to Stay in Park City: A Complete Guide to the Best Locations

October 26, 2025 • By Randy Kirsch

Park City, Utah, is a world-renowned mountain destination offering exceptional skiing, charming historic streets, and a lively après-ski atmosphere. But choosing where to stay can make all the difference in how much you enjoy your trip.

From the slopeside access of Park City Mountain Village to the charm of Main Street and luxury of Deer Valley, each area has its own appeal. Here's a guide to the five main areas and our pick for the best all-around place to stay.

1. Park City Mountain Village (The Base)

Best for: True ski-in, ski-out convenience and being in the heart of everything

If you're looking for the best combination of skiing, dining, and convenience, Park City at Mountain Village is unmatched. This is the main hub of Park City Mountain Resort, where the ski school, ticket office, rentals, and five major chairlifts are located. Everything you need—from coffee shops and restaurants to après-ski lounges and gear shops—is right outside your door.

It's got the perfect location for staying slopeside — with skiing literally at your door — and it's still incredibly easy to reach Park City's Main Street restaurants and nightlife by walking, driving, or hopping on the free city shuttle. It truly is the best of all worlds, offering both direct mountain access and seamless connectivity to downtown.

It's also the central stop for the free Park City shuttle, so you can explore Main Street or Deer Valley in minutes without needing a car.

Why Park City's Mountain Village Wins:

  • Unbeatable ski-in, ski-out access to multiple lifts
  • Walkable restaurants, shops, and après-ski options
  • Central transportation hub for the entire area
  • Easy access to Main Street by foot, car, or shuttle
  • Ideal for families, couples, and groups
  • Ice Skating Rink
  • Lots of options: Lodge at Mountain Village, The Lowell, Park City station
  • The most vibrant, convenient location in all of Park City

2. Main Street (Historic Old Town)

Best for: Dining, nightlife, and shopping

Main Street is Park City's historic heart—lined with boutiques, art galleries, and world-class restaurants. It's perfect for visitors focused on dining and entertainment rather than full days on the slopes.

While the Town Lift provides access to the mountain, it's a very slow lift. Waiting fo the lift or carrying ski gear can be cumbersome, and parking is often expensive or limited.

Pros:

  • Excellent restaurants and nightlife
  • Historic charm and pedestrian atmosphere

Cons:

  • Slow, limited ski mountain access via Town Lift
  • Traffic and noise, especially on weekends or during Sundance
  • High prices for smaller and older accommodations
  • Parking challenges

If you're serious about shopping and restaurants, you'll likely prefer staying on main Street.

3. Canyons Village

Best for: Resort-style luxury and family-friendly amenities

The Canyons area has seen rapid development and offers a modern, upscale feel. With full-service hotels, slopeside dining, and ski-in/ski-out access, it's a strong option.

However, if you plan to ski the Park City side frequently or visit Main Street often, it can feel remote. Traveling between the two sides of the mountain takes time by gondola or shuttle.

Pros:

  • Newer, luxury lodging options
  • Full-service amenities
  • Shared lift pass with Park City Mountain

Cons:

  • Farther from Main Street and the Park City Mountain skiing, feels remote
  • Longer transfers between mountain areas

4. Deer Valley Resort

Best for: Luxury travelers and fine dining

Deer Valley is synonymous with upscale skiing. Known for impeccable grooming, limited lift tickets, and high-end service, it's perfect for guests seeking sophistication and calm. Dining options on-mountain are exceptional, and service is top-notch.

Pros:

  • High-quality service and food
  • Fewer crowds due to ticket limits

Cons:

  • Premium pricing
  • No snowboarding allowed
  • Limited nightlife and shops; requires a drive to Main Street
  • Its own destination, its remote from park City

If you are looking for elegant and refined, Deer Valley is your area!

5. Prospector Square

Best for: Budget-friendly accommodations and local convenience

Prospector is a few minutes east of downtown and offers solid value for travelers who don't mind commuting to the slopes. It has easy access to the free shuttle and many casual restaurants nearby.

Pros:

  • Affordable lodging
  • Good local dining options
  • Convenient shuttle access

Cons:

  • Not walkable to lifts
  • Least "ski-town" vibe area, remote form the Base
  • Shuttles can be crowded during peak hours

Prospector is practical and affordable, though it lacks the ski-in/ski-out energy of the resort areas.

The Verdict: Park City's Mountain Village Is the Best of All Worlds

When you weigh ski access, dining, family-friendliness, and convenience, Park City Mountain Village stands out as the clear winner. It's the only area where you can step directly onto the slopes, walk to restaurants, and easily explore all of Park City without ever needing a car.

Whether you're visiting for world-class skiing, a family vacation, or a romantic mountain getaway, staying at the Base gives you more of what makes Park City so special.
